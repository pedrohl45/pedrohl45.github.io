From the ERP price to the shelf label: release at checkout only after the ACK

Case study · internship project at ITNEX · Python, FastAPI, MQTT, ESP32-S3, Bluetooth LE

Context

Electronic shelf labels (e-paper) show a product's price. The price comes from the ERP, and the label receives the image over Bluetooth LE through a gateway. I worked on a platform of our own to cover that whole path, without depending on the manufacturer's cloud.

The problem

  • The label price and the checkout price must match. If the checkout charges the new price before the label shows it, the customer sees one value and pays another.
  • The label is a slow, unreliable device: the BLE update can fail or take a while, and there is no delivery guarantee.
  • Little can be tested with real hardware, which is slow, scarce and shared with other projects.

What I decided

1. A price increase is only valid at checkout after the label's ACK

The backend receives prices from the ERP, renders the image, sends it through the queue and releases the price at the point of sale only when the label confirms it received it. Until there is a confirmation, the checkout keeps the old price, which is the one the label still shows.

2. Our own gateway and broker

An ESP32-S3 acts as the BLE gateway and speaks MQTT with a broker I control. The flow doesn't depend on a third-party service in the middle, and every message can be observed.

3. Small layers

  • Backend (FastAPI + MQTT): prices from the ERP, send queue and release at the point of sale.
  • Renderer: turns the image into the exact bytes of the e-paper panel.
  • Driver: commands from the renderer to the gateway.
  • Gateway: the ESP32 that talks to the labels over BLE.

4. A gateway simulator for end-to-end tests

I wrote a simulator that answers like the gateway, with a local broker. The full flow (price, image, send, ACK, release) runs without a label at hand, and the test can be repeated as often as needed.

5. Measure before deciding

Before deciding how and when to connect to the labels, I measured the connection behavior on the bench. The decision came from measurement, not assumption.

Result

  • A complete flow, from the ERP to the label, with the price release conditioned on confirmation.
  • Each layer can be tested and replaced on its own, and the simulator allows testing the whole cycle without hardware.
  • The project is in the bench phase, not in production yet.

What I learned

  • With a device that has no delivery guarantee, confirmation has to be part of the design. You can't assume it was sent and arrived.
  • A simulator pays for itself early: testing time drops and hardware stops being the bottleneck.
  • Bench measurements prevent nice-looking decisions on paper that don't work on the real device.
